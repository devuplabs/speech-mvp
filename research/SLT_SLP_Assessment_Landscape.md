## 1. Executive Summary

**The problem in plain language.** When a speech and language therapist evaluates a child or adult, they rarely "wing it." They reach for a **standardized assessment** — a published, commercially produced test (forms, stimulus books, scoring keys, a manual) that has been administered to a large, representative sample of people so that any individual's performance can be compared against that reference group. The CELF (Clinical Evaluation of Language Fundamentals) that prompted this report is the archetype: a clinician administers fixed subtests in a prescribed way, converts the child's raw answers into **standard scores, scaled scores, and percentiles**, and then writes a report interpreting those numbers. The *report* is bespoke to each clinic; the *assessment* is standardized and identical everywhere it is sold.

**Why standardization matters.** Standardized, norm-referenced tests give three things informal observation cannot: (1) an **objective benchmark** ("this 7-year-old scores like a typical 5-year-old"), (2) **defensible eligibility decisions** (school funding, special-education placement, disability services), and (3) **measurable progress** over time. Because the scores carry real consequences, publishers **restrict who can buy and administer the tests** (see qualification levels in §2), and reviewers scrutinize their psychometric quality (reliability, validity, sensitivity/specificity).

**How therapists actually use them.** A typical pathway: *screen* (quick pass/fail to decide who needs more) → *diagnostic assessment* (a full norm-referenced battery) → *interpret & report* (standard scores, percentiles, qualitative observations) → *re-assess* (progress monitoring). Different instruments are purpose-built for different points in that pathway, and for different **domains** (language, speech sounds, fluency, voice, swallowing, cognition, etc.) and **ages** (preverbal infants through to adults with stroke or dementia).

**How the landscape differs by country.**

| | United Kingdom | United States | Canada |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulator** | **HCPC** (mandatory; "speech & language therapist" is a protected title) | State licensure boards | **Provincial colleges** (e.g., CASLPO in Ontario, OOAQ in Québec) |
| **Professional body / guidance** | **RCSLT** | **ASHA** (Practice Portal) | **SAC / OAC** (Speech-Language & Audiology Canada) |
| **Dominant norms** | Prefers **UK-normed** editions (CELF-5 UK, BPVS3, Renfrew, NRDLS) | **US-normed** (CELF-5, PLS-5, GFTA-3) | Largely **US-normed tools via Pearson Canada**; CELF-5 has a Canadian-English edition; **French/bilingual** assessment is a recognized gap |
| **National battery?** | No — clinician-led per RCSLT guidance | No — IDEA requires "a variety of technically sound tools" | No — set provincially |

The single most important cross-country theme: **the same instrument often ships in multiple regional editions with different normative samples.** CELF-5 exists in US, UK, Canadian-English, and Australian/NZ versions — *the test items overlap, but the norms differ*, and using the wrong country's norms undermines the validity of the scores.

## 2. How to Read This Report

### 2.1 Glossary of key terms

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Standardized test** | A test administered and scored in a fixed, consistent way so results are comparable across people and settings. |
| **Norm-referenced** | Performance is compared to a representative **normative sample** ("norms"). Produces standard scores, percentiles, etc. Answers *"how does this person compare to peers?"* |
| **Criterion-referenced** | Performance is judged against a fixed standard or skill checklist, not against other people. Answers *"can this person do X?"* Common for early-communication, AAC, and many swallowing tools. |
| **Raw score** | The simple count of correct items before any conversion. Not interpretable on its own. |
| **Standard score** | A raw score converted onto a scale with a fixed **mean (usually 100) and standard deviation (usually 15)**. ~68% of people score 85–115. The headline score on CELF, PLS, etc. |
| **Scaled score** | A subtest-level standard score, typically **mean 10, SD 3** (range ~1–19). Subtests combine into composite standard scores. |
| **Percentile rank** | The % of the norm group a person scored at or above. Percentile 16 ≈ 1 SD below the mean; percentile 50 = average. |
| **Standard deviation (SD)** | A measure of spread. On a mean-100/SD-15 scale, a score of 70 is 2 SD below the mean — a common cut-off for "disorder." |
| **Age-equivalent** | The age at which a given raw score is *average*. Intuitive but psychometrically weak; most manuals warn against over-reliance. |
| **Basal / Ceiling** | The point where testing starts (basal — items below assumed correct) and stops (ceiling — consecutive errors). Defines how much of a test each person takes. |
| **Confidence interval** | A band around a score reflecting measurement error (e.g., "SS 82, 90% CI 77–87"). |
| **Sensitivity / Specificity** | Sensitivity = % of truly affected people correctly identified; specificity = % of unaffected people correctly cleared. Key for screeners. |
| **PRO (patient-reported outcome)** | A self- (or proxy-) completed questionnaire capturing the person's *own* perception (used heavily in voice and stuttering, e.g., VHI, OASES). |

### 2.2 Qualification levels — who is allowed to buy restricted tests

Most clinical tests are **restricted**: the publisher requires proof of professional qualification before selling. The dominant scheme is **Pearson's A/B/C system** (used in the US; the UK/Canada operate equivalent registration systems). *(The exact level for any given test should be confirmed on the product page — levels below are the general framework and were not all individually fetch-verified.)*

| Level | Typical requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Level A** | No special qualification — open purchase. | Some informal checklists, basic forms. |
| **Level B** | A master's degree in a relevant field (SLP/SLT, psychology, education, OT) **or** professional certification (e.g., ASHA CCC, HCPC registration, RCSLT membership) **or** relevant training in test administration. | **The vast majority of SLT/SLP tests** — CELF, PLS, GFTA, CASL, WAB-R, etc. |
| **Level C** | An advanced/doctoral qualification or license in psychology, **or** documented advanced training in test interpretation. | Some neuropsychological/cognitive instruments; certain Bayley uses. |

**Publisher-specific notes:**
- **Pearson (UK)** uses a registration/competency system mapped to professional status; SLTs are HCPC-registered, which satisfies the requirement for most speech-language tests.
- **Pro-Ed** uses its own qualification statement (often described as Level A/B equivalents); buyers self-certify professional status.
- **WPS** uses Levels A/B/C very similarly to Pearson.
- **GL Assessment (UK)** and **Routledge/Speechmark** restrict clinical tests to SLTs, educational psychologists, or specialist teachers.
- **MoCA** now requires **mandatory training + certification** to administer (a notable change — see §3.9).
- **MBSImP** requires holding the **ASHA CCC (or equivalent)** plus paid certification training (see §3.11).

- **Resonance** (hypernasality/cleft palate) uniquely relies on **instrumental nasometry** (Nasometer II) for objective nasalance scores, alongside perceptual rating.
- Most voice PROs are **free for clinical/research use**, though VHI is technically ASHA-copyrighted (free non-commercial use; commercial reuse needs permission).

## 4. Country Deep-Dives

### 4.1 United Kingdom

- **Regulation:** **HCPC** (Health and Care Professions Council) registration is **mandatory**; "speech and language therapist" / "speech therapist" are **protected titles** (misuse can incur a fine up to £5,000). HCPC sets the Standards of Proficiency.
- **Professional body:** **RCSLT** (Royal College of Speech and Language Therapists) — publishes clinical and outcome-measurement guidance (incl. the **ROOT** outcome tool) but does *not* regulate. RCSLT advocates combining standardized measures with informal/functional assessment, and **explicitly cautions against over-reliance on standardized tests** for culturally/linguistically diverse children.
- **Dominant / UK-normed instruments:** **CELF-5 UK**, **CELF Preschool-3 UK**, **PLS-5 UK**, **BPVS3**, **Renfrew Language Scales (RAPT, Bus Story)**, **NRDLS**, **ACE 6-11**, **TROG-2**, **DEAP** (articulation/phonology), **PhAB2 / YARC** (literacy), **CAT / PALPA / FAST** (aphasia), **FDA-2** (dysarthria), **VoiSS / VTD** (voice).
- **National battery?** No mandated battery; selection is clinician-led, with a strong preference for **UK norms**.
- **Key sources:** [RCSLT guidance](https://www.rcslt.org/guidance/) · [HCPC SLT standards](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/standards/standards-of-proficiency/speech-and-language-therapists/) · [Pearson Clinical UK speech & language](https://www.pearsonclinical.co.uk/featured-topics/speech-language.html)

### 4.2 United States

- **Regulation:** State licensure boards license SLPs; most also hold the **ASHA CCC** (Certificate of Clinical Competence).
- **Professional body:** **ASHA** (American Speech-Language-Hearing Association) — its **Practice Portal** provides assessment guidance.
- **School eligibility (IDEA):** In schools, assessment determines eligibility under the IDEA "**speech or language impairment**" category. IDEA requires *a variety of technically sound, non-discriminatory tools, administered in the child's native language by qualified personnel*; screening alone is not an evaluation; and ASHA does **not** support "cognitive referencing."
- **Dominant instruments:** **CELF-5** (school-age), **PLS-5** (early childhood), **GFTA-3** (articulation), **CTOPP-2** (phonological processing), **PPVT-5/EVT-3** (vocabulary), **CASL-2 / OWLS-II / TOLD** (language); **WAB-R, BDAE-3** (aphasia), **RBANS, CLQT+** (cognitive-communication), **MBSImP/FEES/VFSS** (dysphagia).
- **National battery?** No federal battery — governed by IDEA's general requirements + ASHA guidance, with CELF-5/PLS-5/GFTA-3 the de-facto core.
- **Key sources:** [ASHA Practice Portal](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/) · [ASHA school eligibility](https://www.asha.org/slp/schools/prof-consult/eligibility/) · [Pearson Assessments US](https://www.pearsonassessments.com)

### 4.3 Canada

- **Regulation:** **Provincial colleges** regulate practice — e.g., **CASLPO** (College of Audiologists and Speech-Language Pathologists of Ontario) and **OOAQ** (Ordre des orthophonistes et audiologistes du Québec). Membership is required to perform evaluations. CASLPO's consolidated practice standards (covering assessment of children/adults, acquired cognitive-communication, dysphagia, stuttering) took effect Sept 2025.
- **National body:** **SAC / OAC** (Speech-Language & Audiology Canada / Orthophonie et Audiologie Canada) — national association and certification, publishes guidance (incl. on linguistically diverse clients), but does not license.
- **Norms & bilingual issue:** Canada relies heavily on **US-normed tools via Pearson Canada** (CELF-5, PLS-5, GFTA-3). **CELF-5 has a Canadian-English edition.** A recognized challenge: **Québec/French and bilingual assessment** lack population-specific norms for many tools, so clinicians use US norms *with explicit caution* (addressed in OOAQ guidance).
- **Dominant instruments:** Same core as the US — **CELF-5, PLS-5, GFTA-3** — plus Canadian-developed cognitive-communication tools like **FAVRES** (CCD Publishing) and the Canadian-origin **MoCA**.
- **National battery?** No — set provincially; SAC provides national guidance.
- **Key sources:** [SAC regulatory bodies](https://www.sac-oac.ca/joining-the-profession/regulatory-bodies/) · [CASLPO SLP standards](https://caslpo.com/registrants/standards-and-resources/slp-practice) · [Pearson Clinical Canada speech & language](https://www.pearsonclinical.ca/en/products/speech-language.html)
