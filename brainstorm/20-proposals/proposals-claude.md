## Candidate 1: Section F tribunal report engine

- **One-liner:** A per-report drafting-and-QA engine that turns an independent SLT's assessment data into a tribunal-defensible EHCP Section F contribution with full evidence provenance; used by SLTs, funded out of the £900–1,400 report fees parents already pay them.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** SEND tribunal appeals hit 25,002 in 2024/25 (+83% in 24 months) with a ~99% parent win rate ([Disability Rights UK](https://www.disabilityrightsuk.org/news/send-tribunal-appeals-record-high)); hearings are booked into 2028. Only 46.4% of EHCPs are issued inside the statutory 20 weeks. Parents demonstrably pay £900–1,400+ for private SLT tribunal reports today ([TheSENDList cost calculator](https://thesendlist.co.uk/private-send-assessment-cost-calculator/), [Flourish SLT](https://flourishslt.uk/ehcp-and-send-tribunal-reports/)) — real money moving now, not stated intent. Section F must specify and quantify provision to be enforceable ([RCSLT position paper](https://www.rcslt.org/wp-content/uploads/media/Project/RCSLT/ehc-plans-and-slt-provision.pdf)), and LA-side AI drafting is facing documented parent-community backlash for boiler-plate output ([Special Needs Jungle](https://www.specialneedsjungle.com/risks-benefits-artificial-intelligence-power-ehcps/), [Lancashire](https://lancashire.thelead.uk/p/lancashire-county-council-send-assessment-ehcps-parents-unhappy-ai-tool-reports)) — which raises the value of the therapist-authored, provenance-backed report. Forcing events: statutory deadlines and hearing dates. ASSUMPTION: drafting time (6–10 hrs/report) is the throughput bottleneck limiting how many report fees an independent SLT can capture.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** The independent SLT practice. Budget line already exists: the report fee itself (the SLT keeps £900–1,400; we take £40–75/report, or £150/mo for 4 reports). Card-payable, no procurement.
- **Distribution:** (a) **EHCP tribunal solicitors and advocates** — small education-law firms publicly complaining they can't get expert reports fast enough ([Education Law Advice](https://www.educationlawadvice.com/2026/06/18/educational-psychologist-shortages-and-ehcp-delays-what-parents-need-to-know/)). Gatekeeper: firm partners; referral economics make them market us for free (faster reports = more winnable cases). Near-zero vendor saturation per R3. (b) **Found and admin the "SLTs doing EHCP work" community** (WhatsApp/Circle) — no such admin-owned niche community exists; owning it is defensible, guesting in SLT Facebook groups is not. Incumbents don't own either: Beam/Invision360/Agilisys sit on the LA side and are structurally distrusted by this side of the market; scribes don't do statutory formats. Two people can win it: the whole UK surface is ~1,500 independent SLTs doing this work plus a few dozen boutique law firms, and our design partner is already inside it.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Data-eval + community + compliance. A growing golden corpus of *what survives tribunal cross-examination* (wording, quantification patterns, provenance chains) that no scribe or LA vendor can collect from their side; admin-ownership of the practitioner community; an append-only audit trail that makes every sentence traceable to assessment evidence — a compliance artifact, not a feature.
- **Asset leverage:** Platform (review-gate, audit-by-construction) transfers ~80%; domain depth transfers fully (design partner writes these reports); eval harness partially — the 7 encoded statutory frameworks become an automated specificity/quantification checker; compliance posture covers child-data DPIA.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Per-report SLT tooling → the same QA checker sold to the four LA-side EHCP-AI vendors and then to LAs themselves (R4's O1 prize, entered with credibility from the therapist side) → OT/EP disciplines, which share the statutory format.
- **Riskiest assumption:** That drafting throughput, not assessment capacity or appetite for tribunal work, is what caps independent SLTs' report income.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Concierge. Via the design partner and one solicitor introduction, recruit 5 SLTs; they send anonymised assessment data, we return a Section F draft with provenance table in 48h at **£40/report, invoiced from day one**. Success: ≥8 paid reports across ≥3 clinicians and ≥2 clinicians send a second batch unprompted within 14 days. Kill: <4 paid reports, or zero repeat batches. Cost: ~£100 tooling.

## Candidate 2: SLT-Bench — the assurance layer

- **One-liner:** A public safety benchmark for AI tools operating in SLT/SLP workflows (scribes, plan-drafters, EHCP tools), monetised as paid specialty-validation engagements sold to the vendors; SLTs use the free leaderboard to choose safe tools and cite it in their own clinical-governance paperwork.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** Vendors already pay £5–25k per clinical safety case to CSO consultancies, and compliance SaaS (Assuric, Naq) sells today ([Assuric](https://www.assuric.com/dcb0129), [Naq](https://www.naqcyber.com/blog/clinical-safety-officer-requirements-under-dcb-0129)) — the invoice exists. DTAC 2.0 became the mandatory NHS procurement gate on 6 Apr 2026; DCB0129/0160 are being revised specifically for AI risk (consultation closes 11 Sep 2026) ([NHS Digital](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-standards/governance/latest-activity/standards-and-collections/review-of-digital-clinical-safety-standards-dcb0129-and-dcb0160)); MHRA's AI Airlock is funded £1.2m/yr to 2029. Meanwhile splose (AUD $46M, UK entry), Jane (UK scribe launch) and Heidi (70k NHS clinicians) are all shipping into allied health with zero allied-health-specific safety evidence — existing benchmarks are US-physician-centric with no UK-statutory or allied-health content (R4/A1; [npj Digital Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01895-6)). Beam proved published evidence *is* the UK channel.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Clinical-AI vendor (head of product/compliance), paying from the compliance-consultancy/QA budget they already spend. £5–15k per specialty validation engagement; plus a £499 self-serve, card-payable "benchmark your tool" tier so first revenue needs no procurement. Leaderboard free.
- **Distribution:** **Publish-the-yardstick.** We release the leaderboard, methods, and 10 open personas; press, RCSLT (which has AI principles but no evaluation — [RCSLT](https://www.rcslt.org/slt-voices/shaping-the-future-in-a-digital-age/)) and vendor marketing teams cite it. Gatekeeper: none — we own the site, the scoreboard, and the email list of tool-evaluating clinicians it collects. Why empty: no one else has a jurisdiction-aware allied-health corpus; building one takes clinician-months incumbents won't spend. Why 2 people win: the asset already exists in CI; the channel is publication, not sales headcount.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Eval/benchmark asset plus yardstick position — once two vendors are scored, the rest must submit to *our* measure or explain why not; each engagement compounds a private corpus of failure modes. Compliance credibility ("we run the stack we assure") reinforces it.
- **Asset leverage:** Eval harness is the entire product (54 personas, 18 specialties, 8 jurisdictions, golden expectations, deterministic CI); compliance posture is the credibility; domain depth authors new goldens; platform contributes the CI/report machinery.
- **Wedge → expansion:** SLT-specialty validation of scribes → "safety case as code" aligned to the revised DCB0129/0160 (respond to the consultation before 11 Sep — free authority) → allied-health-wide assurance via the persona methodology → NHS AVT procurement evidence packs.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Vendors pay *before* regulation bites — that leaderboard exposure plus procurement pressure converts to purchase orders in 2026 rather than 2027.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Week 1: run 5 named tools through 10 personas; publish the scorecard + methods. Week 2: direct outreach to 10 vendors (splose, Jane, Heidi, WriteUpp, Zanda, Ambiki, the 4 EHCP-AI vendors) offering an early-access full-specialty audit at £1,500 with a **£500 card-payable deposit**. Success: ≥1 paid deposit, or ≥2 vendors formally submit their tool for scoring under agreement (committed behaviour with reputational cost). Kill: 10 contacts, zero money, zero formal submissions. Cost: ~£150 (hosting, API calls).

## Candidate 3: Waiting-list referral engine

- **One-liner:** A free "while-you-wait" intake-and-triage tool for parents of the ~67–75k children on NHS SLT waiting lists; independent SLTs pay per triaged, intake-complete referral delivered to their diary.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** 66,695 children waiting for SLT, ~46-week average waits ([TheSENDList](https://thesendlist.co.uk/nhs-speech-language-therapy-waiting-times-uk/)). Money is already moving from the queue to the private sector: parents pay £77–100+/session ([SpeechWise](https://speechwisetherapy.co.uk/our-prices/)) and NHS services are outsourcing (one service transferring 900 CYP to independent providers, R4). On the supply side, independent SLTs already spend on patient acquisition — ASLTIP membership fees buy a "find a therapist" directory listing ([ASLTIP](https://asltip.com/)); ASSUMPTION: many also run Google/Facebook ads. Buying a referral is buying revenue, which satisfies R2's revenue-framing rule in the only way a practitioner purchase can: one £30 referral converts to a client worth £500–2,000+ in session fees.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Independent SLT / small clinic owner; budget line = existing marketing/directory spend. £25–50 per qualified referral or £49/mo for a postcode pipeline. Card-payable, zero procurement. Parents pay nothing, ever.
- **Distribution:** **SEND parent communities + programmatic SEO** — "SLT waiting time [town]", "help while waiting for speech therapy", EHCP timeline queries. Per R3 this is the single most mispriced channel: near-zero vendor saturation, £0–low entry, hostile to ads but warm to genuinely free help; gatekeepers are volunteer group admins and charities, who will share a free, non-commercial-feeling tool. It is structurally invisible to the competitor: their capture is clinician-side, and R3 rule 4 says the best pivot makes that irrelevant — they own the clinician's notes; we own the clinician's *next patient*. Two people can win it because it's content + a free tool, not a sales force; the owned asset is the parent email list and account base.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Distribution ownership (the parent audience) plus two-sided lock-in in each local market once SLTs depend on the pipeline; regional demand data no incumbent has. This is the OpenEvidence/Heidi free-tool pattern applied to the demand side.
- **Asset leverage:** Highest platform reuse of any candidate — parent intake → triage is exactly the half of the MVP the scribes didn't build; eval harness triage goldens gate safety (red-flag presentations, e.g. swallowing concerns, get signposted to NHS urgent care, not a referral); compliance posture (child-data DPIA, UK residency) is what earns charity/admin endorsement; domain depth shapes the triage.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Referral fees → remain the intake/triage front-door the SLT keeps using post-referral (Cliniko write-back occupies a *non-scribe* integration slot, per R3) → with usage evidence, an ICB waiting-list validation/triage product in year 2 (R4's O4, entered with data instead of a cold DTAC slog).
- **Riskiest assumption:** Independent SLTs have spare capacity and currently pay to fill it. If overflow demand already fills their diaries organically, referral supply is worthless (and the flip risk: if parents won't complete a structured intake for a free tool, there's nothing to sell).
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Week 1: launch a landing page + waiting-time explainer for 2–3 pilot postcodes; share with permission in 3 SEND parent groups; £150 ad top-up. Week 2: offer local independent SLTs the first 3 triaged referrals for a **£25 card deposit each**. Success: ≥100 completed parent intakes AND ≥5 SLTs pay deposits. Kill: <2 SLTs pay despite available referrals (supply-side dead), or parent intake completion <2% of visitors (channel dead). Cost: ≤£300.

## Candidate 4: Pay-per-analysis scoring lab (US-first)

- **One-liner:** A pay-per-use language-sample-analysis and assessment-interpretation engine for US SLPs: upload a transcript or recording plus raw scores, get back LSA metrics, norm-referenced context, and clinician-reviewed report-ready interpretation paragraphs.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** US SLPs already pay per-use for scoring and analysis — the invoice exists: Pearson's Q-global scoring subscriptions run ~$40/user/year *per assessment* ([Q-global subscriptions flyer](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/q-global/q-global-unlimited-subscriptions-flyer.pdf), [Q-global](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/en-us/Store/Professional-Assessments/Q-global-Web-based-Administration%2C-Scoring%2C-and-Reporting/p/100000680)), Q-interactive is usage-priced ([pricing sheet](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/q-interactive/qi-pricing.pdf)), record forms are paid consumables, and SALT sells paid clinical licenses for LSA with no modern web competitor ([SALT 24 Clinical License](https://www.saltsoftware.com/salt-24-clinical-license)). R1 confirms assessment scoring/analysis is whitespace — Ambiki's articulation analysis is the only production instance found. Note the distinction rivals will probe: US school-SLP *planning/materials* is saturated (SLP Now et al.); *scoring and LSA* is not.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Individual SLP or small private clinic, card-payable, from the assessment-consumables/scoring-software budget they demonstrably hold. $8–12 per analysis or $19/mo; districts later, never first.
- **Distribution:** **Programmatic SEO via free calculators** — "MLU norms calculator", "type-token ratio calculator", "words-per-minute norms" — queries with real clinician volume and no vendor ownership (Pearson won't cannibalise consumables; SALT is desktop-era with no SEO presence; scribes don't score). Gatekeeper: none — Google plus an owned email list captured by the free tier. US podcast/newsletter spots (SLP Nerdcast etc.) used only as rented top-ups funnelling into the free tool, per R3 rule 5. Two people can win it: it's a content-and-tool play with zero sales motion, and HIPAA posture is already built.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Data-eval — a growing golden corpus of graded LSA analyses and interpretation exemplars (the "what good looks like" asset R2 says acquirers underwrite) plus SEO/audience ownership. Deliberately avoids publisher IP: we compute LSA metrics (research-literature norms) and generate interpretation narrative from clinician-entered standard scores; we never reproduce proprietary norms tables.
- **Asset leverage:** Eval-harness *methodology* (schema-validated personas, golden expectations, deterministic CI) transfers to LSA goldens; platform review-gate and audit trail transfer; HIPAA-aligned terraform finally earns its keep in the US; domain depth partially (paeds).
- **Wedge → expansion:** Per-analysis tool → the assessment-evidence layer feeding IEP present-levels and eligibility documentation (US) and statutory reports (UK, joining Candidate 1) → district site licences once individual adoption creates internal pull.
- **Riskiest assumption:** That solo-clinician revealed spend on Pearson/SALT transfers to a new per-use vendor — R2 warns solo clinicians are the hardest buyer, and this is the one candidate exposed to that risk; it lives or dies on repeat purchase, not signups.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Concierge with a paywall on the *second* use: offer "language sample analysed in 24h, first one free" via r/slp and one SLP newsletter (~£200); second analysis costs **$8 by Stripe**. Success: ≥10 paid analyses from ≥8 distinct SLPs within 14 days (free→paid ≥10%). Kill: <5 payers, or free-to-paid conversion <10% — that would replicate the exact solo-buyer failure that triggered this pivot, and the candidate dies. Cost: ≤£300.
