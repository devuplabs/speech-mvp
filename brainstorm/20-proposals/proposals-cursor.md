# Pivot proposals — Cursor

## Candidate 1: Speech AI Assurance Lab

- **One-liner:** An independent, SLT-specific regression-testing and safety-evidence service for UK and US clinical-AI vendors, paid for by the vendors and institutional buyers whose products touch SLP workflows.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** In England, compliance with DCB0129/0160 is required under the Health and Social Care Act, and NHS England is revising both standards specifically because AI creates new clinical risks ([NHS England](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-standards/governance/latest-activity/standards-and-collections/review-of-digital-clinical-safety-standards-dcb0129-and-dcb0160)). Vendors already buy DCB0129 safety-case work from specialist providers such as [Assuric](https://www.assuric.com/dcb0129) and [Naq](https://www.naqcyber.com/blog/clinical-safety-officer-requirements-under-dcb-0129), while generic physician-centric benchmarks provide no allied-health or UK statutory coverage. That is existing compliance spend and a condition of NHS revenue, not stated clinician interest. **ASSUMPTION:** a vendor will redirect part of that spend to specialty performance evidence before an NHS, school, or clinic-group buyer explicitly mandates it.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Product, clinical-safety, or regulatory lead at a scribe, PMS, teletherapy, or SEND-AI vendor; paid from QA, clinical safety, regulatory, or enterprise-deal enablement budgets. **ASSUMPTION:** start with a fixed £2.5k–£10k SLT evaluation sprint rather than another per-seat SaaS subscription.
- **Distribution:** Founder-led outbound to the finite list of vendors already entering allied health, plus a public “SLT AI safety benchmark” that makes vendors come to be measured. The gatekeepers are vendor clinical-safety/product leads and institutional procurement teams. The incumbent that captured clinician channels is a prospective customer, not a channel competitor. Two people can run a services-led evaluation from a reusable harness and publish one benchmark; they do not need to win thousands of clinicians.
- **Moat (non-feature):** A growing, clinician-graded, jurisdiction-labelled failure corpus; reproducible benchmark methodology; longitudinal vendor results; and citations in safety cases and procurement packs. Each paid engagement adds test cases and calibration data that a generic model vendor cannot recreate from product features alone.
- **Asset leverage:** The separate speech-ml 54-persona, 18-specialty, 8-jurisdiction eval harness is the product seed. The platform has draft-review fields, audit primitives, and a compliance posture, but only parent-summary publishing currently completes a review/sign-off flow; a generic attestation endpoint and evidence-pack exporter are net-new. Most of the parent-facing UI is discarded, and PHI case routes must be wrapped in the existing auth middleware before the app can credibly demonstrate the controls it evaluates.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Sell one UK DCB0129-oriented SLT eval sprint; then recurring regression certification for releases, US school/clinic procurement scorecards, synthetic test-data licensing, and additional allied-health specialties.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Vendors feel the specialty evidence gap before a buyer or regulator names SLT-specific testing in a contract; if generic safety paperwork remains sufficient, this becomes an unfunded “nice to have.”
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Over 14 days, spend under £100 producing a five-case sample report from the existing harness and contact 20 named clinical-AI/PMS safety leads. Offer a £2,500 sprint with a non-refundable £500 booking deposit. Success is one paid deposit and access to a real product build; kill or radically reframe if 20 qualified decision-makers yield no deposit.

## Candidate 2: Statutory Report Exchange

- **One-liner:** A secure referral and production network that routes UK SEND-tribunal and US IEE cases to vetted SLTs/SLPs, with the parent, solicitor, advocate, or school system paying per case.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** UK families already pay roughly £600–£1,500 for an independent SLT tribunal report, and one live practice requires a £900 initial payment ([The SEND List](https://thesendlist.co.uk/private-send-assessment-cost-calculator/), [The Orchid Practice](https://theorchidpractice.co.uk/tribunal-services/)). In the US, IDEA gives parents a right to an independent educational evaluation at public expense when they dispute a school evaluation; the district must fund it or defend its evaluation in due process ([IDEA §300.502](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/e/300.502)). These are already-funded, deadline-driven reports, not optional admin tools.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** UK parents or their legal representative from tribunal-case spend; US local education agencies from IEE/special-education budgets, or families paying privately. Sona charges a 12%–15% case fee or a £150–£250 secure-intake, scheduling, and report-workflow fee; the clinician retains the clinical fee.
- **Distribution:** Build referral agreements with SEND solicitors/advocates in the UK and special-education law firms and parent-training centres in one US state. Those partners gatekeep high-intent cases and need reliable expert capacity. PMS and scribe incumbents own clinician attention, not legal referral demand. Two people can start as a concierge with one tribunal-experienced design partner and a small vetted panel before automating anything.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Exclusive/referral relationships, a credentialled expert network, observed turnaround and case-fit data, and a clinician-graded corpus of report defects and statutory requirements. Reliability and network liquidity compound; a report-writing feature does not.
- **Asset leverage:** Parent intake links, clinician availability/booking, multi-tenancy, legal-hold support, audit logging, and PDF reports transfer. A `reviewedAt` field and FHIR review gate exist, but clinical-report drafts cannot currently be edited or signed off through the API; statutory templates, attestation, and expert governance are net-new. The current report generator is generic and can fall back to stubs, and PHI case routes need production auth before reuse.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Concierge-match five English SEND cases to one to three expert SLTs; then add one US state's publicly funded speech-language IEEs, hearing availability, annual-review evidence, and eventually institutional overflow contracts.
- **Riskiest assumption:** A two-person company can recruit enough genuinely tribunal/due-process-qualified clinicians and manage conflicts, licensure, safeguarding, and liability without becoming a low-margin staffing agency.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Over 14 days, spend under £200 on secure checkout and outreach; with the design partner's capacity pre-confirmed, ask ten specialist solicitors/advocates to route one suitable live case through a secure intake and charge a non-refundable £150 booking fee before any build. Success is one paid booking plus clinician acceptance; kill the channel if ten qualified referral conversations produce zero deposits. No PHI goes in email or the landing page.

## Candidate 3: Speech Authorization Recovery

- **One-liner:** A US-first, SLP-specific prior-authorization and denial-recovery service that assembles medical-necessity evidence from clinical records, paid by clinic owners from cash actually recovered.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** In ASHA's November 2024 Vermont survey, 43% of SLPs reported payment reductions and 59% reported access problems caused by Medicaid prior authorization; ASHA's 2026 New Hampshire submission also notes that 82% of analogous Medicare Advantage appeals overturn initial denials ([ASHA/NHSLHA](https://www.asha.org/siteassets/advocacy/comments/asha-letter-to-new-hampshire-on-rule-he-w-568_03272026.pdf)). Connecticut now requires attestations or IEP evidence plus at least four treatment notes for some pediatric speech authorizations, and incomplete requests are cancelled ([CT Provider Bulletin 2025-38](https://www.ctdssmap.com/CTPortal/Information/Get-Download-File?Filename=pb25_38.pdf&URI=Bulletins%2Fpb25_38.pdf)). Practices already buy full-scope RCM at about 6%–10% of collections, proving a budget line for authorization and denial work ([2026 RCM cost guide](https://medicalbillingrates.com/revenue-cycle-management-services/)).
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Owner or operations lead of a 5–30 clinician US speech/therapy practice; paid from existing RCM, billing-staff, or lost-revenue budgets. Price at 10%–15% of recovered cash with a $250–$500 minimum diagnostic/setup fee, not a promise to “save admin time.”
- **Distribution:** White-label through small therapy-specialist billing firms and consultants that submit claims but lack SLP-specific medical-necessity evidence; the gatekeeper is the billing-firm owner or clinic operations lead. Generic RCM vendors and PMS scribes own transaction processing and notes, not the cross-setting clinical-evidence layer. Two people can begin with one denial reason, one payer, and one state as a manual service.
- **Moat (non-feature):** A payer- and state-specific rule graph linked to actual appeal outcomes, plus a de-identified corpus showing which evidence reverses which speech-therapy denials. Integrations and accumulated authorization history create workflow/data lock-in.
- **Asset leverage:** Structured intake, longitudinal progress entries, audit controls, and HIPAA-aligned infrastructure transfer. The existing export proves a mapping pattern but supports UK Core only; US FHIR profiles, clinical-report edit/sign-off, production auth on PHI case routes, claims submission, eligibility checks, payer integrations, and denial workflows are net-new. The persona harness can become a regression suite for medical-necessity packets only after the concierge wedge proves recovery.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Recover one recurring pediatric Medicaid denial class in one state; then add payers and states, prevention before submission, and partner distribution through RCM firms. **ASSUMPTION:** the evidence engine could later support UK SLTs contesting LA/NHS spot-commissioning decisions, but UK funding workflows are different and should not be treated as a copy-paste expansion.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Speech-therapy claim values and appeal win rates leave enough recovered cash after labor, and existing RCM firms will partner instead of copying the narrow workflow.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Over 14 days, spend under £200 on targeted outreach and secure onboarding; recruit five practices in one state, execute appropriate BAAs, and sell a $250 paid denial audit plus a contingent-recovery agreement using records shared only through an approved secure channel. Success is two paid audits and at least $2,500 of facially recoverable claims identified; kill if no practice pays the diagnostic fee or the recoverable pool cannot support a 10% success fee.

## Candidate 4: SLP Capacity Clearinghouse

- **One-liner:** A managed UK/US exchange that sells fixed blocks of assessment, report, supervision, or telepractice capacity from credentialled SLTs/SLPs to schools, clinic groups, and public-service contractors.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** DC Public Schools contracted supplemental SLP capacity at $100/hour in person and $95/hour virtually for the 2025–27 period ([DCPS contract](https://dcps.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dcps/page_content/attachments/GAGA-2026-C-0046%28A%29_Contract_Amergis_TWHsigned.pdf)). In England, an Ealing therapy-services report describes a £1.718m annual contract, about 60% of spend flowing through spot commissioning or routes outside council control, and an SLT waiting list exceeding 500 children ([Ealing Council](https://ealing.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s23068/ICMD%20Report%20Recommissioning%20of%20Therapy%20Services.pdf)). Buyers already pay agencies and independent providers because IDEA/EHCP provision and waiting-list duties cannot be deferred.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** US district special-education directors, UK specialist schools/independent providers, and multi-site clinic owners; paid from contract staffing, spot-commissioned therapy, or vacancy-cover budgets. Charge a 10%–15% fulfilment fee or a £250/$300 retained-search fee credited against the first block.
- **Distribution:** Monitor public SLP service RFPs and sell smaller overflow blocks through specialist-school groups, clinic directors, and regional staffing firms that cannot fill every niche. Procurement leads and clinical directors are the gatekeepers. The competitor's clinician-software channel is irrelevant; broad staffing incumbents optimise for FTE placements, leaving short assessment/report/supervision blocks fragmented. Two people can manually clear one geography and one service type.
- **Moat (non-feature):** A verified credential/licensure, specialty, availability, and completion graph on both sides of the market; repeat buyer contracts; and service-quality data. Liquidity in hard-to-fill micro-blocks is the moat, not matching software.
- **Asset leverage:** Parent intake, manual triage records, clinician availability, one-off consult booking, multi-tenant data structures, audit trails, and legal holds transfer; the AI drafting layer is not required. The platform lacks production auth on PHI case routes, credentialing, cross-tenant assignment, recurring sessions, payments, contracting, and telepractice licensure logic, so this is a meaningful operational pivot rather than a relabel.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Clear ten fixed-scope assessment/report slots for one UK provider or one US district/clinic group; expand to supervision of assistants, short-term absence cover, multi-state telepractice where lawful, and then recurring managed-capacity contracts.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Supply, licensure, safeguarding, worker classification, and clinical-liability constraints allow profitable case blocks; incumbent agencies may already fill the most attractive demand.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Over 14 days, spend under £100 on direct outreach; contact 15 buyers with visible vacancies/backlogs and secure one non-refundable £250 retained-search fee for a defined ten-slot block, while obtaining written availability and rate commitments from at least three appropriately credentialled clinicians. Success requires both the paid retainer and viable supply; kill if either side fails, even if interviews are enthusiastic.

## Candidate 5: AI Safety Simulation CPD

- **One-liner:** Case-based AI-safety simulations for UK SLTs and US SLPs, paid by clinicians, employers, universities, or continuing-education providers as accredited CPD/CE.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** US SLPs must complete 30 professional-development hours every three years for ASHA certification maintenance, including ethics content, and ASHA already sells a school-SLP AI ethics course for $59 to members/$77 to non-members and identifies it as a popular 2026 course ([ASHA course](https://apps.asha.org/eWeb/OLSDynamicPage.aspx?Webcode=olsdetails&title=Practical+and+Ethical+Integration+of+AI%3A+Empowering+the+School-Based+SLP)). UK SLTs must continuously maintain CPD, declare compliance at two-year HCPC renewal, and may enter the 2.5% audit sample ([HCPC guidance](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/globalassets/resources/guidance/continuing-professional-development-and-your-registration.pdf)). Existing course purchases prove the training budget; **ASSUMPTION:** buyers will pay more for performance-tested simulation than for another lecture.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Individual clinicians from mandatory CE/CPD spend; school/clinic employers from learning-and-development or clinical-governance budgets; or an established CE provider under revenue share. Start at £39/$49 per live seat or £1.5k for an employer cohort.
- **Distribution:** Co-brand with one ASHA-approved CE provider, UK CPD provider, or university course lead; they gatekeep accreditation and an owned learner list. Clinician-software incumbents do not own this formation channel, and existing AI courses mostly teach concepts rather than test performance on risky outputs. Two people can deliver live cohorts before building a learning platform.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Accredited-provider relationships, a proprietary bank of clinician-graded simulations, longitudinal competency data, and an employer-recognised credential. The content gets harder to copy as learner errors improve the cases and scoring rubrics.
- **Asset leverage:** The persona/eval harness can be repackaged into realistic “spot the unsafe output” simulations across specialties and jurisdictions; compliance material and the design partner support facilitation. The transactional clinical platform transfers little, which keeps the initial build small. No licensed assessment norm tables are used or reproduced.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Run a live “Can you catch unsafe clinical AI?” cohort using 12 existing personas; then accredited on-demand modules, employer competency dashboards, university licensing, and vendor-sponsored training that does not compromise scoring independence.
- **Riskiest assumption:** This is a useful cash-flow and audience wedge but may cap out as a course business; accreditation delays or free professional-body content could erase pricing power.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Over 14 days, spend under £100 using static case materials, a checkout page, and a video call; secure a CE/CPD provider distribution partner and pre-sell a 90-minute cohort at £39/$49. Success is at least five paid seats and one signed partner/revenue-share agreement; kill as a standalone pivot if fewer than three of 50 qualified landing-page visitors pay. Survey answers and free registrations do not count.
