# Pivot proposals — Codex-slot substitute (Claude, contrarian framing)

**Author stance:** I accept the evidence standards in PROTOCOL.md fully. What I do not accept is the gravitational pull of the research toward UK statutory buyers and compliance-as-product. The memos are excellent and, I suspect, overfit: they treat "statutory budget exists" as the trump card and quietly discount the two things a 2-person bootstrapped team actually needs — **weeks-not-months to first cash, and a buyer who pays by card**. Three of my four candidates are US-weighted or self-serve on purpose: the US has ~218,000 SLPs to the UK's ~2–4k independents, it has statutory forcing events every bit as brutal as EHCPs (IDEA's 60-day evaluation clock, Medicaid audit exposure), and nobody on this council was likely to propose selling there because the founders are UK-based. That is founder-comfort bias, not strategy. All claims new to this document carry citations; claims already evidenced in R1–R4 are marked as such.

---

## Candidate 1: US evaluation-report engine, per-report, self-serve

- **One-liner:** A card-payable, per-report AI engine that turns standardized test scores + clinician observations into a defensible, review-gated SLP evaluation report (CELF-5, GFTA-3, PLS-5 …), sold directly to US school and private-practice SLPs at $5–15/report.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** US SLPs *already pay real money* for exactly this job, at exactly this price shape: Evalubox sells evaluation-report generation at **$5/credit pay-as-you-go or $12.50–20/mo** ([Evalubox](https://www.evalubox.com/), [Evalubox support](https://help.evalubox.com/article/144-managing-your-subscription-in-evalubox)); easyReportPRO has sold CELF/CASL report software for years ([sample report](https://www.easyreportpro.com/PDF/SLP_CELF_CASL.pdf)); SLP-specific note tools charge $29.99/mo and generic scribes $39–119/mo that SLPs pay personally ([SOAP Note Buddy comparison](https://soapnotebuddy.com/best-ai-scribe-speech-therapy/), [Freed pricing](https://www.getfreed.ai/pricing)). The forcing event is statutory, not vibes: IDEA imposes a **60-day evaluation timeline** ([Disability Rights California](https://www.disabilityrightsca.org/publications/special-education-timelines)); districts are being sued and sanctioned for missing it — Austin ISD fell **800+ evaluations behind** ([KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/investigations/court-records-aisd-behind-on-more-than-800-special-education-evaluations/)) and settled with a **$4M compensatory fund plus court-ordered compliance deadlines** ([KUT](https://www.kut.org/education/2024-09-05/austin-independent-school-district-special-education-lawsuit-disability-rights-texas-settlement)); Cumberland County faces the same ([CityView](https://www.cityviewnc.com/stories/lawsuit-state-say-cumberland-county-schools-delays-special-education-evaluations/)). Evaluation reports are the throughput bottleneck the scribe products structurally miss — a scribe transcribes a session; it cannot interpret psychometrics. R1 itself flagged "assessment scoring & analysis" as thin whitespace, then the research walked past it toward UK councils.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** The SLP herself (card; displaces/tops the $5–30/mo she already spends per revealed prices above), and secondarily her district under evaluation-backlog duress. Price: $10/report or $19/mo unlimited — inside the revealed envelope.
- **Distribution:** **Named channel: US SLP podcast/creator sponsorships + owned email list.** Gatekeepers are individual creators (SLP Now, SLP Coffee Talk, SLP Nerdcast) who sell sponsorship for $500–5k/episode and are hungry for sponsors (evidenced in R3's channel map). The incumbents saturate *planning/materials* in this channel, not evaluation reports; Evalubox is small and does not own any creator relationship visibly. Two people can win it because the buy-in is hundreds of dollars, the niche creators answer their own email, and one genuinely good free tool (a score-interpretation calculator: "paste your CELF-5 scaled scores, get the interpretation paragraph") converts listeners into an owned list. This is Freed's segment-arbitrage playbook (R3 case study) applied one workflow deeper.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Data/eval asset + community. A growing golden corpus of *clinician-graded* "what a defensible evaluation report looks like" per test instrument and per state's eligibility criteria is exactly the kind of accumulated structured clinical data R2 §5 says acquirers underwrite — and it compounds with every reviewed report. Feature parity is copyable; a graded corpus plus a creator-channel position in a niche this unglamorous is not worth a $65M scribe's time.
- **Asset leverage:** Platform review-gate + audit trail transfer whole (an evaluation that feeds an IEP meeting benefits from provenance); HIPAA-aligned terraform finally gets used for its stated purpose; the eval-harness *methodology* (schema-validated personas, golden expectations) transfers — the 54 UK-weighted personas partially do (2 of 8 jurisdictions are already US-relevant; ASSUMPTION: extending goldens to 5 major US test instruments is 3–4 weeks of clinical-partner work). Domain depth transfers at the discipline level, not the jurisdiction level.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Per-report engine → the system of record for the US evaluation-to-IEP pipeline (eligibility justification, IEE-grade independent reports, district throughput contracts once the self-serve base proves the corpus). Same intake→triage→plan skeleton, new jurisdiction.
- **Riskiest assumption:** That two UK founders can produce US-clinically-credible content fast enough without a US SLP partner — and that Evalubox's small footprint indicates an immature market rather than a small one.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Concierge, no new code. Recruit via one $300–400 newsletter/podcast shoutout + free Facebook-group-legal score-calculator page. Offer: "Send scores + notes, get a full draft evaluation report in 24h, $12/report, first one $5." **Success:** ≥10 distinct paying US SLPs and ≥3 repeat purchases within 14 days. **Kill:** <5 payments, or zero repeats — repeat purchase is the metric, per R2's concierge rules.

## Candidate 2: Expert-report capacity network for tribunal/IEE work

- **One-liner:** A two-sided platform that routes SEND-tribunal (UK) and Independent-Educational-Evaluation (US) report demand from solicitors/advocates to independent SLTs/SLPs, with an evidence-grade report-production engine underneath; monetised per report from day one.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** UK: 25,002 SEND appeals in 2024/25, parents win ~99% (R4, [Disability Rights UK](https://www.disabilityrightsuk.org/news/send-tribunal-appeals-record-high)); hearings listed into 2028 (R3, [Changing Lives SEN](https://www.changinglivessen.co.uk/blog/send-tribunal-appeals-in-england-a-parent-friendly-guide-to-ehcp-disputes-deadlines-and-evidence)); parents already pay **£900–1,400+ per private SLT tribunal report** (R4, [Flourish SLT](https://flourishslt.uk/ehcp-and-send-tribunal-reports/)); expert-witness brokerages already exist and take a margin — INNEG advertises a **panel of 328 SALT expert witnesses** ([INNEG](https://www.inneg.co.uk/expert-panel/discipline/speech-and-language-therapy-expert-witness)) and specialist providers quote **4–6 week turnarounds** as normal ([Expert Court Reports](https://www.expertcourtreports.co.uk/blog/speech-language-therapy-expert-evidence-send-appeals/)). US mirror: IDEA entitles parents to an IEE **at district expense**, and settlements are now *ordering* districts to fund them ([KUT/Austin ISD](https://www.kut.org/education/2024-09-05/austin-independent-school-district-special-education-lawsuit-disability-rights-texas-settlement)). Money is demonstrably moving on both sides of the Atlantic for the same artifact: an independent, litigation-defensible SLT/SLP report.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** Parents via solicitors (UK, existing £900–1,400 spend) and districts under IEE obligations (US). The SLT/SLP is the *earner*: the platform takes a £75–150 per-report fee out of an existing fee, and sells the production engine at £50/report to the clinician — revenue-framing per R2 §4 ("this wins you £1,200 of report work"), not time-saving.
- **Distribution:** **Named channel: education-law firms and SEND advocates (UK) + special-ed attorneys/advocates (US).** R3's map scores this channel *near-zero incumbent saturation, 2-person winnable* — and then the research's recommendations largely ignored it in favour of LAs. Gatekeepers are small firms with referral economics who currently wait 4–6 weeks for reports; whoever reliably delivers a defensible report in 10 days owns the referral flow. The competitor's clinician-channel capture is irrelevant here — this channel doesn't read PMS marketing. Two people can win it by BD calls: there are perhaps 100–200 relevant UK firms; 20 conversations covers meaningful share.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Two-sided network (solicitor demand + vetted clinician supply) plus *provenance as a legal feature*: an append-only audit trail showing exactly what the clinician assessed, reviewed and signed is what survives cross-examination — the one context where Sona's audit-by-construction is a selling point, not plumbing. Deliberately NOT the LA side (R4's O1): serving parents forecloses LAs, and I choose the side that pays in weeks and wins 99% of the time.
- **Asset leverage:** Highest in this document: platform review-gate + audit trail (whole), jurisdiction-aware statutory frameworks in the harness (7 already encoded — Section F goldens are literally the QA layer), design partner as first supply-side clinician and report-quality grader. Compliance posture reassures solicitors on child data.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Brokered tribunal/IEE reports → the standard production tool for all independent SLT statutory reporting (EHCP contributions, annual reviews) → the credentialed "verified independent report" mark that LAs eventually accept — entering the statutory market from the side that's winning.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Supply, not demand: that independent SLTs (21% vacancy economy, fully booked) will take brokered report work at volume rather than treating it as overflow they refuse.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** No code. Week 1: call/email 20 UK education-law firms and 10 SEND advocates offering "SLT tribunal report, 10-working-day turnaround, £950, £100 deposit to reserve a slot" (fulfilled by the design partner + one recruited SLT; ~£100 in call/list costs). Week 2: collect deposits. **Success:** ≥2 paid deposits and ≥5 firms requesting slot availability in writing. **Kill:** zero deposits, or the design partner + 3 approached SLTs all decline fulfilment work — that kills the supply side and the candidate.

## Candidate 3: Clean-claims documentation layer OEM'd through Medicaid billing vendors

- **One-liner:** A white-label, SLP-facing documentation layer that guarantees Medicaid claim-eligible session logs, sold not to 13,000 districts but to the dozen school-Medicaid billing vendors whose contingency revenue depends on claimable documentation.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** School-based Medicaid pays districts **$4–6B/yr** ([US Dept of Education](https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/about/offices/list/osers/docs/medicaid-funding-for-school-based-services-03-08-2024.pdf)), up to $7.5B by other estimates ([Learning Policy Institute](https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/blog/medicaid-more-health-insurance-its-lifeline-public-schools)), and SLP services are a core claimable category ([ASHA](https://www.asha.org/practice/reimbursement/medicaid/schoolbasedservicesslp/)). Money is being *lost* for documentation reasons at audited scale: NYC schools alone failed to collect **up to $431.6M over three years** ([NYC Comptroller](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/newsroom/press-releases/comptroller-audit-finds-nyc-schools-failed-to-collect-up-to-431m-in-medicaid-reimbursements-for-special-education-services/)). An entire vendor industry already exists and is paid **3–25% contingency on reimbursements recovered** ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a246871.html); payment models per [Frontline Education](https://www.frontlineeducation.com/blog/exploring-different-payment-models-for-medicaid-billing-vendors-in-k-12/)) — the invoice R2 rule 1 demands. Poor/incomplete documentation is a primary driver of denied therapy claims ([TheraPlatform](https://www.theraplatform.com/blog/2714/slp-billing-metrics-what-slps-should-track-to-get-paid-accurately), [Dastify](https://www.dastifysolutions.com/medical-billing/speech-therapy/)), and rules vary wildly by state — Florida ~$3.74 vs Virginia ~$31.91 per 15-minute unit ([RelayHub guide](https://relayhub.com/medicaid-reimbursement-for-speech-language-pathology-slp-school-based-services-a-comprehensive-guide/)) — which is precisely a jurisdiction-parameterised-goldens problem.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** The billing vendor (Frontline, GoSolutions-class firms). Their budget line is COGS against contingency revenue: every SLP note their layer makes claimable is direct margin. Price: per-provider-per-month OEM fee ($3–8 PPPM across thousands of district SLPs) or a share of incremental recovered reimbursement. The SLP remains the served user — the constraint is honoured: this tool lives inside the SLP's daily logging workflow.
- **Distribution:** **Named channel: OEM partnership with school-Medicaid billing vendors.** Gatekeeper: vendor product leadership — a *countable* set of companies, reachable by two founders doing outbound in a fortnight. Incumbent scribes don't own this channel because generic ambient notes are exactly what fails claiming rules; the vendors' own tools are forms-first, not AI-assisted. A 2-person team can win it because the sale is B2B2B to ~a dozen accounts, not a district land war — and the vendor brings distribution to tens of thousands of SLPs overnight (the Elklan-style channel-inheritance move R3 §3.3 endorses, applied to the US).
- **Moat (non-feature):** Workflow embedding inside the vendor's rails (R3's highest-defensibility category) + a 50-state claim-eligibility golden corpus maintained under deterministic CI — a compliance/data asset with real upkeep cost that a vendor would rather rent than build, and that transfers vendor-to-vendor.
- **Asset leverage:** Review-gate platform and audit-by-construction transfer whole (Medicaid audits are the tribunal of this candidate); HIPAA-aligned terraform is table stakes finally cashed in; eval harness re-pointed from clinical goldens to claim-eligibility goldens — the methodology, schema and CI transfer even though the 54 personas mostly don't.
- **Wedge → expansion:** One OEM vendor deal → the eligibility-rules engine of record for school billing → direct district product for IEP/eligibility documentation once the corpus and brand exist.
- **Riskiest assumption:** That vendors perceive documentation quality as *their* problem to fix with a partner, rather than the district's problem or a feature to build in-house; and channel-partner sales may stretch past two-person patience even with only a dozen targets.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Outbound only. Build a 5-minute demo from the existing platform (no new code beyond prompts): SLP session note in → state-rule-checked claimable log out, with audit trail. Pitch 12 named billing vendors + 15 district Medicaid coordinators. **Success:** one vendor signs a paid discovery pilot (≥$1,000 deposit invoiced) or an LOI naming price, date and signatory (the only LOI form R2 admits). **Kill:** zero paid commitments AND fewer than 3 vendors granting a second meeting — that means the channel sees no margin in it, and the candidate dies.

## Candidate 4: Agent-app-store land grab: SLP workbench in ChatGPT/Claude

- **One-liner:** The first credible SLP-named app in the ChatGPT app directory / Claude connector ecosystem — free workbench (goal-writing, score interpretation, session-plan drafting on *de-identified* inputs) upselling a card-paid PII-safe Pro tier; SLPs adopt it in an afternoon.
- **Problem & pain evidence:** SLPs are *already* using general-purpose AI for admin tasks — surveyed clinicians report AI use concentrated exactly there ([AJSLP survey](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2024_AJSLP-24-00218)) — i.e., the behaviour exists and is being serviced badly (no SLP structure, no PII safety) by a £20/mo ChatGPT subscription they already pay for (revealed spend, R2 §2.1). The distribution substrate is new and empty: OpenAI's Apps SDK is live with in-chat commerce arriving via Stripe-powered Instant Checkout and developer monetisation announced ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-apps-in-chatgpt/), [ChatAds overview](https://www.getchatads.com/blog/what-is-chatgpt-apps-sdk/)); public submissions opened Dec 2025 (R3, [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-now-accepting-chatgpt-app-submissions-from-third-party-devs-launches)); R3's own channel map scores clinical-niche naming real estate "near zero" saturated — then the research shelves it as a "lottery ticket." I think that's wrong: for a 2-person team whose whole problem is distribution, a zero-CAC channel with first-mover ranking in front of the platform where SLPs already do this work is not a lottery ticket, it is the cheapest channel experiment on the board.
- **Buyer of record & budget line:** The SLP, by card, $19–29/mo Pro — priced inside the ChatGPT-Plus/SOAP-Note-Buddy envelope she demonstrably pays ([SOAP Note Buddy $29.99](https://soapnotebuddy.com/best-ai-scribe-speech-therapy/)). Pro = the things a prompt can't be: PII-safe processing under a BAA/UK-GDPR posture, saved caseload context, exportable audit-trailed artifacts.
- **Distribution:** **Named channel: the ChatGPT app directory + Claude/MCP connector directory**, gatekept by platform review — cheap to enter (days of dev), unclaimed in this niche, with ranking/naming compounding for early entrants (R3 §1). The competitor's PMS and community capture is *irrelevant inside the chat window*. Free tier is the marketing budget (R3 rule 5); rented creator mentions funnel into the app, not a pricing page.
- **Moat (non-feature):** Speed + first-mover directory position + compliance posture as the paywall. Any team can copy features; almost no 2-person team holds UK-residency/HIPAA-aligned infrastructure that turns "PII-safe mode" into a one-click upsell. The durable asset is the owned user list harvested from a platform channel before it gets crowded.
- **Asset leverage:** Compliance posture (the paywall), platform API + review-gate (Pro backend), harness (regression-tests every prompt behaviour before shipping — speed with safety); domain depth shapes the toolset. The Flutter front-end dies; good riddance.
- **Wedge → expansion:** Directory app → owned list of thousands of AI-forward SLPs → cross-sell Candidate 1's evaluation engine into that list. (Candidates 1 and 4 compound: same buyer, two channels.)
- **Riskiest assumption:** Platform policy: OpenAI/Anthropic may restrict health-adjacent apps or change ranking/monetisation, and free-chat users may convert at ~0 when "good enough" is the free host model. This is a channel bet, explicitly.
- **Validation test (≤2 weeks, ≤£500):** Week 1: ship a minimal MCP/Apps-SDK workbench (goal bank + score interpreter; de-identified inputs only; dev time is the cost). Week 2: one $300 creator shoutout; in-app "Founding Pro — $19/mo, PII-safe mode at launch" Stripe link (real charge, refundable). **Success:** ≥300 activations and ≥10 real card payments. **Kill:** <5 payments, or platform rejection of the app — either falsifies the channel, which is the thing being tested.

## Where I dissent from the research

First, I believe R4's top-ranked "safety-case/eval compliance" lane is a consultancy trap dressed as a product — the buyer pool is low-hundreds of vendors buying one-shot engagements, the closest US analogue (CHAI's assurance labs) already collapsed per R4's own citation, and the harness is worth more as the *invisible quality engine* inside candidates 1–3 and as a published benchmark-for-credibility (R3 §3.6) than as the company itself. Second, the memos systematically underweight the US: they document a 100x-larger SLP population with statutory forcing events (IDEA 60-day clocks, IEE obligations, Medicaid audits) and card-paying revealed spend at every price point, then route every recommendation through UK councils and NHS frameworks whose sales cycles nearly killed this company once already. Third, where the research treats self-serve and agent-platform channels as side bets, I'd argue they are the main event for a 2-person team — the £500 tests above settle this empirically inside a month, and the money, not the memos, should pick the winner.
